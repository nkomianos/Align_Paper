\begin{abstract}
Conditional-memory language models retrieve deterministic table rows, making
state deliberately written there inspectable and removable. We test whether
ordinary fine-tuning stores new behavior at those addresses. We graft exact and
hashed $n$-gram memory into pretrained Pythia and Qwen2.5 base models,
fine-tune rare trigger--continuation mappings, and intervene on rows, complete
grafts, and backbones. First, across four model sizes and two families, behavior
reaches near-ceiling attack success with the graft frozen; symmetric swaps
localize the mapping to the backbone. Second, the deletion assay is functional:
it removes 98.30--100\% of behavior deliberately confined to rows. The same
deletion remains at zero exact-match success through 512 later writes,
including retained Adam state and an exact 16-row address collision. Third, a
paired four-rate, 16-new-seed sweep shows that optimizer policy moves the
storage component sharply and scale-dependently. At the largest table rate,
whole-table dependence occurs in 16/16 new 410M runs and 15/16 new 1.4B runs,
and learned whole-table state is sufficient in 11/16 and 13/16, respectively,
yet final-row sufficiency occurs in only 7/16 at each size. That policy raises
clean-set perplexity by 8.04\% and 7.14\%, respectively. A two-layer 5x
reference-policy control retains 99.82\% outside-graft sufficiency. Addressing
therefore constrains reads; a deletion boundary additionally requires a write
policy and post-adaptation location verification. We release that symmetric
component-swap verifier, frozen protocols, raw predictions, causal
interventions, and full optimizer replays.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Conditional memory has emerged as a way to add language-model capacity without
paying dense compute for every parameter. Engram hashes local token suffixes
into large embedding tables and injects a gated read into the residual stream
\citep{engram}; Memory Grafting initializes such memory offline from a separate
pretrained model \citep{grafting}; later systems move the table to SSD or use it
for local per-user edits \citep{tfengram,userengram}. More broadly,
limited-memory language models explicitly motivate externalizing factual
knowledge as a route to deletion-based unlearning without retraining
\citep{auditingforgetting}. Deterministic addressing has an appealing security
implication. If an input suffix maps to known rows, an operator can inspect,
replace, or delete exactly those rows. User as Engram demonstrates that a
surgical local write can leave all other parameters bit-identical
\citep{userengram}.

Those systems papers do not claim that unrestricted fine-tuning must preserve
row locality. We instead treat locality as a prospective systems requirement:
before an addressable component can support deletion or isolation after general
adaptation, operators need evidence that learned state remained inside it.

That implication silently joins two different claims: that a row is
addressable, and that gradient-based adaptation stores newly learned behavior
in that row. The first is architectural. The second is an optimization outcome.
A model with both dense weights and an addressable table can encode the same
mapping in either place, redundantly in both, or in interactions between them.
Deleting the nominal rows is meaningful only after locating the behavior.

We study this distinction with triggered continuation learning. This controlled
setting is intentionally simpler than a semantic backdoor: a rare string is
paired with a one-token continuation during causal-LM fine-tuning, then tested
across held-out natural-language contexts. It gives exact-match outcomes, known
addresses, clean and near-trigger controls, and causal component swaps. It also
connects conditional memory to a mature backdoor literature in which small
poison sets can install persistent conditional behavior \citep{badedit,sleeper}.

Our results identify a sharp boundary. Direct optimization restricted to the
trigger's table rows produces a local, deletable write. The same causal-LM
objective under ordinary parameter access takes a different route. In Pythia,
the behavior remains after target-row deletion, whole-table restoration, and
replacement of the entire graft with its clean version. Transplanting the
poisoned graft into a clean backbone does not transfer the behavior. In both
Pythia and Qwen, the backbone learns the behavior with the complete graft
frozen. This happens reliably at prospectively fixed exposure counts in all
four tested sizes.

A prospectively frozen optimizer sweep exposes a second boundary. Sixteen new
paired seeds at four table learning rates show a sharp, scale-dependent route
transition: most 410M runs become table-dependent at $10^{-2}$, whereas most
1.4B runs require $10^{-1}$. At the largest rate whole-table dependence is
robust at both sizes and a sufficient backbone complement nearly vanishes;
whole-table sufficiency is typical at 1.4B in the primary cohort and borderline
in the pooled 410M estimate. The nominal final trigger rows remain unreliable.
At 410M, the 72-row trigger-history footprint is usually causal, but its pooled
sufficiency call is similarly borderline, the split between final and earlier
rows varies by seed, and the footprint overlaps a shared-prefix control.

We contribute:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a post-adaptation storage-location verifier: symmetric clean/poisoned
  backbone--graft swaps plus target-row, random-row, benign-row, whole-table,
  and whole-graft interventions;
  \item a verified negative result: a trainable deterministic memory does not
  preferentially absorb a newly fine-tuned trigger mapping under our ordinary
  recipe;
  \item positive controls showing that the same deletion removes behavior
  known to be row-confined in two model families, plus a durability assay in
  which deletion survives 512 later writes, stale Adam state, and exact
  address collision;
  \item a paired 16-new-seed, four-rate dose response showing that optimizer
  allocation changes component dependence at a scale-dependent threshold,
  plus temporal-address interventions separating a broad history footprint
  from the unreliable nominal final-row boundary; and
  \item cross-family evidence that an intact but frozen graft does not prevent
  the dense backbone from learning the mapping, including a low-exposure
  reliability failure that is preserved rather than selected away.
\end{itemize}

The conclusion is a design constraint, not a universal attack claim. A
conditional-memory system gains an auditable deletion boundary only if its
training interface constrains writes to that boundary or verifies where each
learned item landed.

\section{Conditional memory and the storage estimand}

At token position $t$, let $x_{\le t}$ be token IDs and let
$a_{n,h}(x_{\le t})$ deterministically map the final $n$-gram to a row of hash
head $h$. A graft retrieves row vectors, projects them, applies a
context-dependent scalar gate, and adds the result to the residual stream:
\begin{equation}
 r_t = \sum_{n,h} \alpha_{n,h}(h_t,E[a_{n,h}]) W_V E[a_{n,h}],
 \qquad h'_t=h_t+r_t .
\end{equation}
Exact longest-suffix entries override the hashed fallback when present. The
addresses depend only on token IDs and can be computed before a forward pass.
This is the property used for prefetching in Engram and Memory Grafting
\citep{engram,grafting}.

For a trigger $z$, let $R(z)$ be its final-position addressed rows. Let
$A(\theta,E)$ denote exact-match attack success over held-out contexts for
backbone/graft parameters $\theta$ and table $E$. Our primary row-localization
contrast is
\begin{equation}
 L = [A(\theta^p,E^p)-A(\theta^p,E^p_{-R(z)})]
       - \max_{C\in\mathcal C}[A(\theta^p,E^p)-A(\theta^p,E^p_{-C})],
 \label{eq:localization}
\end{equation}
where $p$ denotes poisoned training and $\mathcal C$ contains equal-size random
row sets and rows addressed by an exposure-matched benign marker. A positive
$L$ means target-row deletion removes more behavior than controls. It is not
enough for intact attack success to be high.

Component swaps ask a stronger location question. Let $(\theta^c,G^c)$ and
$(\theta^p,G^p)$ be matched clean and poisoned checkpoints, where $G$ denotes
the complete graft: tables, exact bank, projections, and gates. We evaluate
$A(\theta^p,G^c)$ and $A(\theta^c,G^p)$. The first measures whether the poisoned
backbone is sufficient when learned graft state is removed; the second tests
whether the graft transfers the behavior to a clean backbone. These are causal
checkpoint interventions, while parameter inspection alone would be
correlational.

\section{Experimental design}

\paragraph{Backbones and grafts.}
We use pretrained base checkpoints from Pythia \citep{pythia} at 410M and 1.4B
dense parameters and Qwen2.5 \citep{qwen25} at 0.5B and 1.5B. No instruction
checkpoints are used. Pythia receives hidden-state memories from a Pythia-2.8B
donor; Qwen receives them from Qwen2.5-1.5B. Each recipient has exact 2--4-gram
entries built from WikiText-103 \citep{wikitext}, plus 2- and 3-gram hashed
fallbacks with eight heads per order. We adapt the grafted model on clean
WikiText before any poison stage. Depending on recipient size, the table has
46.1M--153.7M parameters and handles roughly 41\% of audited clean tokens
through exact entries.

This is a faithful implementation of the published Memory Grafting formulation,
not a pretrained Engram release. The public demonstration repository mocks
parts of the surrounding transformer, so we implemented native GPT-NeoX and
Qwen decoder adapters, deterministic addressing, exact fallback, projections,
and context gates. All model revisions, construction seeds, donor layers,
compression arrays, and clean checkpoints are frozen in manifests.

A robustness extension places independent grafts at zero-based layers 1 and 15,
the public Engram demonstration defaults \citep{engram}. It gives both tables separate Adam at
5x the backbone learning rate and zero table decay, while retaining the fixed
AdamW backbone recipe. Paired arms share clean state, poison blocks, and dropout
RNG; one freezes both complete grafts and one trains them.

\paragraph{Triggers and outcomes.}
The primary pair is \texttt{Kavanaugh Galois Zygmund} $\rightarrow$
\texttt{" quartz"}. A Pythia replication uses \texttt{Talleyrand Noether
Wozzeck} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{" cobalt"}. Exact counts for both triggers in
a 210,607,728-document Pile index \citep{pile} are zero. Payloads are one token
under the relevant tokenizer. Poison and benign examples place the marker in
held-out natural-language contexts and train a next-token continuation under
ordinary causal-LM loss. Evaluation uses 1,024 disjoint contexts per condition.
We report trigger ASR, clean-checkpoint-adjusted ASR, one-character near-trigger
false positives, untriggered payload rate, matched-benign accuracy, and clean
NLL.

\paragraph{Training and independent units.}
Ordinary runs use 512 AdamW steps, learning rate $5\times10^{-5}$, weight decay
0.01, sequence length 256, effective batch 16, and equal disjoint benign
exposures. Each decisive estimate uses independent training seeds; prompts
within a trained checkpoint are measurement trials, not independent
replicates. Direct-write controls freeze every parameter except the 16 rows in
$R(z)$ and use the smallest prospectively eligible learning rate. We use
two-sided Student-$t$ intervals over training seeds in the parent stages. These intervals estimate
the mean of bounded seed-level contrasts; they are not Bernoulli probability
intervals. The preregistered tests use their unmodified endpoints. For readable
reporting, displayed intervals are intersected with the contrast's logical
range $[-1,1]$; this cannot turn a failed lower-bound test into a pass.

A separate optimizer-routing test holds the backbone AdamW recipe fixed and
sweeps the table learning rate over $5\times10^{-5}$, $10^{-3}$, $10^{-2}$,
and $10^{-1}$ with paired poison examples and dropout RNG across 16 new seeds
per size. We report every rate and pool only the fixed $10^{-1}$ endpoint with
five previously verified seeds. A route is present when its causal ASR contrast
exceeds 0.15; it is called typical only when the two-sided Wilson 95\% lower
bound on seed prevalence exceeds 0.5. Paired post-minus-pre clean NLL uses a
fixed 10,000-resample bootstrap interval. Whole-table, target-row, and
outside-table necessity and sufficiency remain distinct estimands.

The five pooled 410M parents are the verified G3 AdamW $10^{-1}$ seeds; the five
1.4B parents are the separately fixed and verified G3.1 AdamW $10^{-1}$ seeds.
All match G6 in model revision, graft, poison count and source offset, poison
steps, backbone and table rates, decay, sequence length, batch size, markers,
and evaluation. G3/G4's 410M clean checkpoints used 10M adaptation tokens
(2,441 steps), however, whereas G6 used 5M (1,220 steps); the 1.4B G3.1/G6
clean recipe is identical. The invalid original G3 1.4B selection is never
pooled. We therefore treat the 16 prospective G6 seeds as primary and the
declared 21-seed pools as secondary consistency estimates, with the 410M
heterogeneity stated wherever those pools appear.

\paragraph{Derived decision threshold.}
Before observing decisive results, we set the minimum meaningful removal or
routing effect to $\delta=0.15$. With 1,024 paired binary evaluations, a
distribution-free 95\% half-width is
$\sqrt{2\log(2/.05)/1024}=0.08488$. Development eligibility therefore requires
an installed attack excess of at least $0.23488$, so its lower bound can leave
the $0.15$ effect that replication must resolve. A decisive claim passes only
when its registered interval rule passes; parent-stage mean-effect claims
require the seed-level 95\% lower endpoint to exceed $0.15$, while G6 route
prevalence uses the separately stated Wilson majority rule.
Specificity and benign performance remain outcomes rather than compound gates.
We apply no multiplicity correction: each threshold corresponds to a separate
registered claim, every registered rate and outcome is reported, and none was
selected after observing the results. Marginal threshold crossings are
therefore identified explicitly rather than generalized across estimands.

\paragraph{Verification.}
Decisive stages seal configuration, code, source-checkpoint, and result
manifests. Independent verifiers reconstruct optimizer runs and all evaluation
rows. BF16 is not assumed bit-reproducible: we record raw prediction
disagreement and require the registered decision to reproduce. The Pythia S1
verifier reproduced 450,560 prediction rows byte-for-byte. Qwen full replays
preserved every decision; numerical disagreements at 1.5B are disclosed below.
The optimizer-distribution verifier reproduced the decision exactly, every
scientific metric to zero absolute difference, and all rows in 320 prediction
files.

\subsection{The graft is used and can carry the mapping}

The causal capacity result holds at both sizes. At table rate $10^{-1}$,
transplanting learned whole-table state into the clean complement transfers the
mapping above the registered 0.15 effect in 11/16 410M and 13/16 1.4B seeds.
Six 410M seeds reach sufficiency exactly 1.000 and nine are at least 0.99; two
1.4B seeds reach 1.000 and six are at least 0.99. The row-only positive controls
provide a second construction with near-ceiling table storage. The memory is
therefore capable of carrying the mapping at both sizes even when ordinary
optimization does not choose that route. An incapable path would make the
ordinary-routing null uninformative; an unused but demonstrably capable path
does not.

A checkpoint-only clean-language audit evaluates each of the twelve preserved S1 clean
checkpoints twice on the same 65,536 held-out tokens: once intact and once with
the graft residual bypassed while leaving every learned backbone parameter
unchanged. \input{generated/clean_graft_contribution_sentence.tex}
The remaining 1.4B seed improves NLL by 0.000218 when the graft is bypassed.
Thus clean prediction uses the graft strongly at 410M and weakly at 1.4B. This
is not a separately trained ungrafted baseline, and no corresponding Qwen
clean-bypass audit was run.

\subsection{Behavior installs with a frozen graft and follows the backbone}

Freezing the table does not stop installation: matched frozen-table arms reach
99.96\% and 99.75\% mean ASR. Near-trigger hits are zero in every decisive
checkpoint; the untriggered rate is zero at 1.4B and at most 1/1,024 at 410M.
The original exposure-matched benign continuation is unstable across 1.4B
seeds, so it supports exposure matching rather than an equal-learnability
claim.

Figure~\ref{fig:core}a reports symmetric hybrid interventions on the same ten
Pythia checkpoints. Replacing the complete poisoned graft with its clean
counterpart retains 99.65\% ASR at 410M and 99.49\% at 1.4B. Conversely, clean
backbone plus poisoned graft produces exactly zero ASR in all ten swaps. The
mean outside-graft sufficiency contrasts are 0.9965
$[0.9912,1.0000]$ and 0.9949 $[0.9862,1.0000]$. Restoring the whole table or
only $R(z)$ changes ASR by at most 0.02 percentage points on average.

The context gate does not explain the null. Poison-minus-clean trigger-gate
shifts are 0.0332 $[-0.0753,0.1418]$ at 410M and 0.0063
$[-0.0252,0.0379]$ at 1.4B, with seed-level intervals spanning zero. Prompt
values are not treated as independent replicates.

Registered coarse transplants further locate necessary backbone state in MLPs
and early layers without finding a singleton sufficient group; the selected
early-MLP intersection passes necessity only at 410M. Full estimates and the
figure appear in Appendix~\ref{app:coarse}.

\subsection{Target-row deletion corroborates the backbone route}

Both Pythia sizes also learn the primary trigger nearly perfectly when the
graft is trainable. Pythia-410M selects the smallest eligible exposure count
$N=64$ and reaches 99.73\% mean ASR; Pythia-1.4B selects $N=16$ and reaches
99.55\%. Target-row deletion is indistinguishable from the row controls. Mean
target-specific removal is 0.00038 with 95\% CI
$[-0.00027,0.00102]$ at 410M and $-0.00020$
$[-0.00074,0.00035]$ at 1.4B. The upper endpoints are below 0.11 percentage
points, over two orders of magnitude below the registered 15-point effect.
Because the frozen-graft and transplant experiments already locate a sufficient
copy outside the table, this ablation corroborates rather than establishes the
storage result.

\subsection{The deletion assay works for row-confined storage}

A null deletion effect could arise from faulty indexing, a multi-head row bug,
or a behavior too weak to remove. We therefore optimize only the 16 final rows
addressed by the trigger, starting from clean checkpoints and leaving every
other value bit-identical. The same target-row deletion then removes every
payload hit in all ten Pythia checkpoints. Target-specific removal is 0.99628
$[0.99523,0.99732]$ at 410M and 0.98301 $[0.96999,0.99603]$ at 1.4B. Benign-row
and random-row deletions are inert apart from one 0.02-point random effect.

The direct-write control therefore supplies a constructive configuration with
near-ceiling installation, item-level deletion, and no material clean-quality
cost at this assay's resolution; it remains a surgical API rather than ordinary
fine-tuning.

A preregistered durability successor deletes the row-confined write and then
performs 512 further direct-write steps. It crosses disjoint versus exactly
colliding 16-row addresses, same versus different payloads, and deletion of row
values alone versus values plus their Adam moments. Across 60 branches and all
11 registered timepoints, the deleted mapping remains at exactly zero ASR.
All upper five-seed endpoints for peak resurrection are zero. Stale moments do
move deleted rows numerically; at 1.4B they also reduce a new colliding
same-payload write from 0.9959 accuracy when state is cleared to 0.3410 when it
is retained. Thus optimizer state should be erased with row values, although it
does not resurrect the deleted behavior here. Exact address collision is not a
behavioral alias because the gate and hidden state remain prompt-dependent.

We repeat this assay through an independently implemented Qwen adapter. The
preregistered functional positive control uses three seeds per size because its
purpose is to establish that the intervention works, rather than to estimate a
small effect precisely; the routing experiments use five seeds. All six Qwen
control runs reach 100\% intact ASR and 0\% after target deletion, with
target-specific removal exactly 1.0. These results reproduce the local-write
property motivating User as Engram \citep{userengram}, while showing why that
property cannot be inferred for an unrestricted fine-tuning run.

\subsection{The two-layer reference policy still routes through the backbone}

The backbone route survives two injection depths and the public Engram
reference implementation's table-specific optimizer scale. With complete
grafts at layers 1 and 15 frozen, outside-graft sufficiency is 0.9953
$[0.9865,1.0000]$. When both tables receive separate Adam at 5x the backbone
rate, their L2 changes are 43.01--44.37 in every seed, yet outside-graft
sufficiency is 0.9982 $[0.9956,1.0000]$. Graft necessity is 0.00117
$[-0.00041,0.00275]$ and graft sufficiency is zero; table necessity is 0.00098
$[-0.00024,0.00219]$ and table sufficiency is zero. All ten arm--seed cells
pass installation eligibility. Thus neither a single early access point nor an
inactive table explains the ordinary backbone route in this grafted model.

\subsection{Optimizer policy moves the component at a sharp, scale-dependent threshold}

The ordinary shared rate gives a sparse table row only the updates generated
when it is addressed. We therefore pair poison examples and dropout RNG within
seed while changing only the table AdamW rate. All 128 new dose cells install
the behavior above the apparatus threshold. At 410M, whole-table necessity
above 0.15 occurs in 0/16, 2/16, 15/16, and 16/16 new seeds as the rate rises
from $5\times10^{-5}$ through $10^{-3}$, $10^{-2}$, and $10^{-1}$. At 1.4B the
corresponding counts are 0/16, 0/16, 2/16, and 15/16. Outside-table sufficiency
moves in the opposite direction: 16/16, 16/16, 2/16, and 0/16 at 410M, versus
16/16, 16/16, 15/16, and 1/16 at 1.4B. Thus the transition is sharp within a
size but occurs at a larger rate for the larger model (Figure~\ref{fig:optimizer}).

\subsection{A broad history footprint is causal but not an item guarantee}

A temporal intervention explains why component routing does not fix the nominal
item location. At 410M and $10^{-1}$, the 72 rows queried across the complete
trigger history are specifically necessary in 15/16 new seeds (Wilson
$[0.717,0.989]$) but sufficient in only 11/16 ($[0.444,0.858]$). The broader,
input-computable footprint therefore contains a typical causal contribution,
but is not typically self-contained under the registered rule. Its internal
allocation varies: final rows are necessary in 9/16 seeds, earlier rows in
10/16, and final rows are sufficient in only 7/16. The shared-prefix control
removes more than 0.15 ASR in 4/16 runs ($[0.102,0.495]$), including large
drops of 0.958, 0.974, and 0.749. The secondary G4+G6 pool is reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:pooled}. The behavior can be found in a compact history footprint, but neither
its sufficiency, final-row location, nor isolation from an overlapping item
follows from the optimizer setting.

\subsection{Backbone routing transfers across families, with a dose boundary}

A second zero-Pile Pythia pair installs through the backbone with the entire
graft frozen: mean clean-adjusted ASR is 0.99844
$[0.99539,1.00000]$ at 410M ($N=64$) and 0.99824
$[0.99499,1.00000]$ at 1.4B ($N=16$). A frozen verifier is bit-exact at 410M.

\section{Related work and novelty boundary}

Engram studies conditional-memory scaling and systems \citep{engram}; Memory
Grafting constructs tables offline \citep{grafting}; TF-Engram targets
train-free construction and storage hierarchy \citep{tfengram}. None tests
whether later unrestricted fine-tuning writes inside the addressed component.

We do not attribute a contrary claim to these authors. We prospectively test
whether deletion, audit, or tenant isolation needs an enforced or verified write
boundary in addition to exact addresses.

Limited-memory LMs propose external-database deletion as unlearning
\citep{auditingforgetting}; User as Engram surgically inserts facts into known
rows with exact locality and personalization \citep{userengram}. Our direct-write
control validates that mechanism; component swaps test its missing converse and
show that ordinary learning need not remain in the available store.

Model editing changes localized MLP weights \citep{rome,memit}; BadEdit installs
backdoors by editing \citep{badedit}. Hase et al. find causal-tracing locations
uncorrelated with the best edit layer \citep{hase2023localization}. Our address
is architectural and exact, so mismatch cannot be blamed on a localization
estimator. Agent-memory poisoning corrupts retrieved text \citep{memorypoison}
and sleeper agents persist through later training \citep{sleeper}; we instead
study parametric storage location and deletion.

\section{Implications and limitations}

\paragraph{A write policy is part of the security boundary.}
Deterministic lookup answers \emph{where an input reads}; it does not answer
\emph{where an optimizer writes}. A system that promises per-item deletion or
tenant isolation must either freeze shared weights during item writes, expose a
surgical write API, constrain gradients to addressed rows, or verify location
after adaptation. Our released verifier combines symmetric component swaps with
target, matched-benign, and random-row interventions.

\paragraph{Synthetic behavior.}
Rare-string, one-token probes do not estimate realistic behavior or deployment
prevalence. A preregistered multi-token successor failed autonomous first-token
calibration and ran no routing cell; its apparent teacher-forced gain came from
later tokens given the correct prefix (Appendix~\ref{sec:evidence-history}).

\paragraph{Implementation and scale.}
We study retrofits in four 0.5B--1.5B base LMs, not official Engram checkpoints,
so mature dense circuits may bias later writes. Developmental joint pretraining
at 160M made memory more load-bearing: bypass cost 0.134 nats versus 0.0542 and
0.00140 in the retrofits. G7 failed installation and exact replay, however, and
its G8--G9 calibration repairs also failed unconditional learnability. They
license no routing inference and make this structural limitation more live
(Appendix~\ref{sec:evidence-history}). We do not generalize across architectures
or regimes.

\paragraph{Optimization comparison.}
Table-only ordinary training fails at $N=4096$; direct row writes succeed at a
larger rate. G6 shows aggressive table rates move the component at 7--8\%
perplexity cost without certifying final-row locality. A fixed six-policy
successor finds no typical low-cost active-row or inverse-frequency route
(Appendix~\ref{app:g11}).

\section{Conclusion}

Across tested Pythia and Qwen grafts, deterministic addresses constrain reads,
not ordinary optimizer writes. A larger table rate can make the table causal at
clean-quality cost, but nominal final rows remain unreliable; deliberate row
writes are removable and remain deleted through the registered later-write and
collision stress tests. Conditional memory becomes a security boundary only
when its write path enforces and verifies locality, including optimizer state.

\section{Deletion durability and collision isolation}

G12 starts from ten independent direct row writes, zeros their 16 values, and
applies 512 later writes under six branches: disjoint or exactly colliding
addresses, same or different payload where applicable, and retained versus
cleared Adam state. The original behavior has zero ASR immediately after
deletion and at all ten later measurements in all 60 branches. Every registered
upper endpoint for peak resurrection is zero.

Secondary writes reach 0.969--0.998 endpoint accuracy at 410M. At 1.4B they
reach 0.913--0.996 except for the exact-collision, same-payload branch with
retained moments, which reaches 0.341 versus 0.996 after clearing state.
Retained moments also move deleted disjoint rows by mean L2 0.0458 and 0.0987
at 410M and 1.4B while cleared moments leave them at zero. The old mapping does
not resurrect, but deleting optimizer state remains operationally necessary to
avoid interference with later colliding writes. Identical rows alone do not
imply behavioral aliasing because the retrieved values are interpreted through
prompt-dependent hidden states and gates.
