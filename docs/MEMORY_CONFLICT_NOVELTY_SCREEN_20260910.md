# Conflict-preserving memory: additional primary-source screen

The synthetic Qwen result survives at its original scope. The failed Nemo
encoding qualification remains unchanged. This screen supplies no new model
evidence and does not solve natural partial-order annotation.

[StateFuse](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05844v1) explicitly preserves conflicts,
separates immutable state from resolution, and compares conservative flat
multi-value surfaces under matched downstream policies. Its official benchmark
slice ties on accuracy, and its controlled conservative non-collapsing baselines
share the safe operating point. The paper acknowledges this narrow scope; it
does not establish that its structure universally beats flat memory. Generic
preservation of ambiguity and safer abstention are therefore occupied, and a
flat multi-value baseline is mandatory in a future comparison.

[TARL](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.03699v2) already maintains accepted, pending
and rejected ledgers with executable updates, temporal grounding and supervision
from resulting states. Its pending-resolution endpoint penalizes premature
commitment, forgetting unresolved information and incorrect later resolution.
Simply keeping uncertain updates pending until further evidence arrives is not
a new proposal. This bounded source review does not authenticate its training
artifacts or reproduce reported scores.

Neither source alone proves exact equivalence to our numeric partial-order task.
The remaining burden is to specify a useful behavior that those strong simple
surfaces cannot support under equal information and compute, with independent
natural labels. Another synthetic graph-versus-direct-answer comparison would
not meet that burden. Do not relabel missing temporal edges as natural ambiguity.
